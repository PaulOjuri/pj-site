# Reference Analysis — paulojuri.com Rebuild
Phase 0 · May 2026

For each reference: one paragraph on **what specific moment we are stealing**, and one sentence on **what we are explicitly not copying**.

---

## rauno.me (Rauno Freiberg)

**What we steal:** The treatment of the site as a precision instrument — every affordance is deliberate, nothing decorates. Specifically: the command bar (Cmd-K) that surfaces keyboard shortcuts everywhere, the micro-interaction on interactive elements where the hover state precedes the click state by just enough to feel physical, and the monospaced meta-information layer (time, location, status) that sits quietly at the edge of the viewport and rewards close reading. We are also stealing the philosophy: the site is a demonstration of the work, not a brochure about the work.

**What we are not copying:** The extreme density and tool-like aesthetic. Rauno's site reads as an OS component — austere, almost cold. paulojuri.com should feel warmer and more editorial. The interaction vocabulary (springs, keyboard shortcuts) is borrowed; the emotional register is not.

---

## emilkowal.ski (Emil Kowalski)

**What we steal:** The physics of cursor interaction — the way interactive elements seem to have mass, pulling the cursor toward them with real inertia rather than snapping to hover states. Specifically: the magnetic button behavior where elements shift slightly toward the cursor before contact, and the way the cursor itself transforms to communicate intent (ring, label, arrow). We are also stealing the restraint: one signature motion per surface, never three competing animations on screen simultaneously.

**What we are not copying:** The monochrome-heavy dark aesthetic or the dense technical widget style. Emil's site is a showcase for motion engineering specifically; paulojuri.com has more content categories and needs a more legible typographic foundation.

---

## olivierlarose.com (Olivier Larose)

**What we steal:** Scroll choreography as storytelling — specifically the technique of pinning a scene while internal elements animate, so the scroll feels like turning pages in a book rather than pushing content off-screen. We want the same quality in case study pages: images that reveal as you scroll, text that lands rather than arrives. We also steal the masked image reveal: a panel sliding away to expose a full-bleed photograph underneath.

**What we are not copying:** The full-canvas page transition that takes 1.5 seconds and covers the entire viewport. Larose can afford it because his site is a portfolio of motion work and the transition *is* the statement. For paulojuri.com, transitions should take ≤ 560 ms and serve navigation, not announce themselves.

---

## brittanychiang.com (Brittany Chiang)

**What we steal:** Typography discipline — the practice of having one font do most of the work, with code/mono used for metadata and labels rather than decoration. The left-side fixed nav on desktop that doesn't fight with the content column. And the principle that a portfolio site should be *scanned*, not read: every section can be understood in three seconds at a glance, with depth available on closer reading.

**What we are not copying:** The muted, green-tinted dark palette — it reads as "developer portfolio" in a recognizable genre. paulojuri.com should be harder to categorize.

---

## josh.comeau.com (Josh Comeau)

**What we steal:** The idea that every article can have bespoke interactive widgets embedded in the prose — not as decoration but as the argument itself. A paragraph about CSS animation behavior can have a live CSS animation embedded in it. This maps to paulojuri.com's `<Demo />` component in case studies: the project page for a browser extension *contains* the browser extension concept, not just screenshots of it. We also steal the reading experience: generous leading, max-width prose columns, typography sized for sustained reading rather than scanning.

**What we are not copying:** The heavy use of rainbow/gradient accent colors and the playful illustrative style. Josh's brand is educator + entertainer. Paulo's brand is practitioner.

---

## linear.app (Linear)

**What we steal:** The confidence of dark mode done seriously. The way shadow and elevation are replaced by border and blur. The motion on interactive elements: everything that moves does so in response to input, never autonomously — except for the most ambient of marquees. And the text treatment on the homepage: a single large headline, enormous, takes up real estate without apology, and is followed by a sub-line that earns the space the headline occupied.

**What we are not copying:** The pure product-marketing framing or the heavy use of 3D hardware renders. Linear's site sells software; paulojuri.com shows craft.

---

## basement.studio

**What we steal:** The editorial confidence of treating the portfolio as a publication — columns, rules, caption-style metadata, numbered projects, the sense that someone deliberate laid this out. The willingness to put large numerals in the background as structural elements rather than content. And the calibration of whitespace: not zen minimalism, but the proportional breathing room of a well-designed magazine spread.

**What we are not copying:** The heavy typographic experiments and the deliberate-illegibility moments. Basement's aesthetic leans into obscurity as a flex; paulojuri.com prioritizes clarity first. The magazine reference is a tone, not a license to confuse.

---

## maxime.dev (Maxime Heckel)

**What we steal:** The practice of using shader-driven backgrounds not as a homepage gimmick but as atmosphere — color and light that respond to the content of the page, not to user attention. Specifically: the way a gradient or a particle field creates mood without demanding to be noticed. We also steal the blog-article-as-world approach: each article can reference its own visual language.

**What we are not copying:** The specific shader aesthetic (deep blues and purples, rave-adjacent glow). The color system for paulojuri.com is designed separately with the skill.

---

## studio-freight.com (Lenis authors)

**What we steal:** Lenis itself — the library is a reference as much as the site. We steal the smooth scroll as an invisible quality signal: a visitor who doesn't know what Lenis is will still feel that something is *right* about how the page moves. We also steal the discipline of shipping motion as infrastructure (Lenis, Tempus) before shipping motion as decoration.

**What we are not copying:** The full-bleed video headers or the heavy scroll-jacking that characterizes studio portfolio sites. paulojuri.com serves content consumers (potential clients reading case studies), not showcase visitors who are there to be impressed by the site itself.

---

## lynnandtonic.com (Lynn Fisher)

**What we steal:** The philosophical permission to be *genuinely weird* — to make a deliberate choice that no one else would make and commit to it fully rather than hedging toward a genre expectation. We don't steal any specific pattern; we steal the posture. The per-project theming spec in §6 is the expression of this posture: each project page should make someone say "I've never seen a case study that looks like this."

**What we are not copying:** The whimsical illustration style or the annual-reskin model. paulojuri.com needs to be maintainable by one person between client projects.
