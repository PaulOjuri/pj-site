# Design Strategy
**paulojuri.com** | Last updated: 2026-05-31

---

## Positioning

Paul Ojuri is a rare practitioner: equal parts engineer and designer, with the credibility to claim both without hedging. Most portfolios hedge — they call themselves "design-minded developers" or "developers who care about UX," which is a way of saying neither. This site does not hedge. The audience is founders and product leads at early-stage companies who need to move fast and ship well — people who have been burned by the gap between designers who can't build and engineers who can't think in systems. They are not browsing for inspiration and they are not comparing vendors. They are trying to decide whether to send an email. The entire site exists to make that decision easy, obvious, and slightly urgent.

---

## Brand Adjectives

**1. Precise**
Not minimalist — minimalism is an aesthetic, and aesthetics can be faked. Precise is a practice: every element on every surface earns its place by doing a specific job, and nothing is there because it looked good in a Figma artboard. Precision is visible in what is absent.

**2. Alive**
The site responds: to scroll, to cursor, to time, to context. Sections reveal as you approach them. The hero reacts to where you are in the page. Project cards carry ambient motion that reflects each project's domain. Static is the enemy of trust — a static portfolio implies a static practitioner, and Paul is not static.

**3. Grounded**
The work is real, the constraints were real, the outcomes are real. Nigeria EMR ran on intermittent power in clinics with no internet. CarbonWise had a specific compliance requirement that shaped every UI decision. Alfera Technik launched on a deadline tied to a trade show. Grounded means the case studies name those constraints, show the decisions they forced, and tell you what happened afterward.

## Design Principles

**1. Motion explains cause and effect; it does not fill silence.**
Every animation on this site has a job: it shows a state change, it guides attention, it reveals hierarchy, or it signals interactivity. If the animation were removed and nothing about comprehension changed, the animation should not exist. Silence — a section that simply appears — is not a problem to be solved with motion.

**2. Each project gets its own visual world; no world bleeds into the shell or into another project.**
The nav, footer, and global shell are painted with global tokens and never change. Inside a project page, the palette, the heading weight, the radius tuning, and the ambient motion posture can all shift to match the project's domain. Prism can feel nocturnal and cool. Alfera Technik can feel material and warm. They do not leak. Switching from one project to another should feel like a deliberate crossing of a threshold, not an inconsistency.

**3. Typography does heavy lifting; illustration and 3D do not substitute for having something to say.**
Fraunces at 96px, set well, carrying a sentence that is actually worth reading, will outperform any decorative 3D hero that says nothing. We use WebGL on the home page because it earns attention and then pays it back — it is not decoration, it is atmosphere. If the WebGL hero were removed, the headline would still work. That is the test.

**4. Every case study is a long-form artifact worth reading end to end; we do not optimize for three-second skims at the expense of depth.**
Founders who might hire Paul are not skimming for a logo grid. They are trying to understand how he thinks under pressure, what he chose not to build, and whether his instincts match theirs. Three-second skim optimization produces case studies that are actually worthless to the people most likely to convert. Write the full story. Trust the reader.

**5. Keyboard and cursor interactions are equally considered; there are no hover-only states.**
Every state that is accessible by cursor is accessible by keyboard. Focus rings are visible, styled, and coherent with the design — not the browser default and not hidden. This is not just an accessibility requirement; it reflects the fact that Paul builds product interfaces, and product interfaces must work for all users.

**6. One signature detail per surface, perfected; not mediocre attention spread across many.**
The home page has the magnetic headline. The project index has the ambient tint system. Each case study has its threshold moment. The contact page has the big email. Choosing one thing to be exceptional at per surface is harder than adding five things that are merely good, and it is what separates authorship from assembly.

## Voice

Paul's written voice is direct, specific, and confident without performing confidence. It does not use em dashes to gesture at sophistication. It does not say "passionate about" or "I believe" or "at the intersection of." It makes claims and lets the work verify them. It treats the reader as someone busy and smart.

**Rewrites:**

Before: "Building at the intersection of code, craft, and culture."
After: **"Software that works the way people think."**
Why: The original is a gesture. The rewrite is a claim. It is also specifically about software — not about Paul, about what he makes.

Before: "Products, tools, and systems I've built, shipped, or contributed to since 2020."
After: **"Six years of shipping. Here's what survived."**
Why: "Contributed to" is weasel language that muddies authorship. "What survived" signals that there is selection, taste, and honesty about failure — all of which are things a founder hiring someone cares about. It is also two sentences that land like one punch.

## Target Audience Profile

**Primary:** Founders and product leads at early-stage companies (seed to Series A). Technical enough to appreciate implementation decisions but not building UI themselves. They have shipped enough product to know what a good engineer-designer hybrid is worth. They are evaluating Paul as a person, not a deliverable.

**Secondary:** Design or engineering hiring managers at mid-stage product companies who want to expand their team with someone who does not require a handoff process.

**Not the audience:** Agencies looking for a contractor who will execute wireframes without judgment. Companies who need someone to fill a defined role without opinion. Design competitions. Other designers.

---

## Conversion Path

The site has one conversion action: send an email to hello@paulojuri.com. There is no contact form. There is no calendly embed on the first touchpoint. There is no "request a quote" flow. There is an email address, presented with confidence, on a page that makes sending it feel like the obvious next step. Every other page on the site exists to earn the moment when someone opens their email client.

**Availability context:** Paul is available Q3 2026, based in Turnhout, Belgium. The site should communicate this clearly — not buried in a footer, but stated plainly in the about and contact pages. Founders plan ahead. Available Q3 2026 is not a deterrent; it is information that makes Paul look in demand and worth planning for.
